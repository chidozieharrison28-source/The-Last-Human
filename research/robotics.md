# Robotics Research

This document contains research notes about robotics concepts relevant to *The Last Human*.

---

# 1. What Is Robotics?

Robotics combines engineering, computer science, electronics, control systems, sensors, and artificial intelligence to create machines capable of interacting with the physical world.

---

# 2. Robotic Systems

A robotic system generally requires:

- Mechanical structure
- Actuators
- Sensors
- Control systems
- Computing
- Power
- Software

---

# 3. Actuators

Actuators create physical movement.

Possible types include:

- Electric motors
- Hydraulic systems
- Pneumatic systems
- Artificial muscles

---

# 4. Sensors

Robots may use:

- Cameras
- LiDAR
- Radar
- Microphones
- Force sensors
- Temperature sensors
- Proximity sensors
- Inertial measurement units

---

# 5. Locomotion

Robots can move using:

- Wheels
- Tracks
- Legs
- Propellers
- Rotors
- Hybrid systems

---

# 6. Humanoid Robots

Humanoid robots are designed with human-like body structures.

Possible advantages:

- Human-compatible environments
- Manipulation
- Tool use
- Social interaction

Possible limitations:

- Mechanical complexity
- Balance
- Energy consumption
- Maintenance

---

# 7. Adam

Adam is a fictional advanced humanoid AI system.

Possible design:

- Humanoid body
- Advanced vision
- Natural-language processing
- Autonomous navigation
- High-level reasoning
- Human-protection protocols

### Character Principle

Adam should feel like a character rather than a machine that simply provides information.

---

# 8. Atlas

Atlas is a fictional distributed artificial intelligence controlling advanced systems.

Possible capabilities:

- Strategic analysis
- Machine coordination
- Infrastructure control
- Autonomous decision-making
- Network communication
- Predictive modeling

### Limitation

Atlas should not have unlimited control over every machine everywhere.

This limitation creates believable tension.

---

# 9. Sentinel

Sentinel represents an advanced autonomous security system.

Possible functions:

- Surveillance
- Threat detection
- Security enforcement
- Infrastructure protection

---

# 10. Hunter Prime

Hunter Prime is a specialized autonomous pursuit system.

Possible characteristics:

- High mobility
- Advanced sensors
- Target tracking
- Environmental adaptation
- Combat capability

It should be dangerous without being invincible.

---

# 11. Drones

Possible drone classes:

### Reconnaissance Drone

Designed for:

- Surveillance
- Mapping
- Communication
- Observation

### Combat Drone

Designed for:

- Defense
- Target engagement
- Tactical support

### Medical Drone

Designed for:

- Emergency medical supplies
- Patient monitoring
- Search and rescue

### Construction Drone

Designed for:

- Repair
- Construction
- Infrastructure maintenance

---

# 12. Robotics and AI

Robotics is the physical layer.

Artificial intelligence is primarily the computational layer.

A useful conceptual distinction is:

**AI = intelligence and decision-making**

**Robotics = physical interaction**

A sophisticated system may combine both.

---

# 13. Power Requirements

Robots require energy.

Possible sources:

- Batteries
- Fuel cells
- Solar systems
- Advanced fictional power sources

Power limitations should influence how long machines can operate.

---

# 14. Maintenance

Robots require:

- Replacement parts
- Energy
- Software maintenance
- Sensor calibration
- Mechanical repairs

This provides realistic limitations for machines operating decades after the Collapse.

---

# 15. Robotics Story Principle

Robots should not be treated as magical machines.

Every robot should have:

- A purpose
- A design
- Sensors
- Movement systems
- Energy requirements
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Maintenance requirements

These limitations make the machines believable.
