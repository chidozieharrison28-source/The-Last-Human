# Computer Vision Research

This document contains research notes about computer vision concepts relevant to *The Last Human*.

---

## 1. What Is Computer Vision?

Computer vision is a field of artificial intelligence concerned with enabling computers to interpret information from images and video.

---

## 2. Cameras and Sensors

Robotic systems may use multiple sensors, including:

- RGB cameras
- Infrared cameras
- Thermal cameras
- Depth cameras
- LiDAR
- Radar

Different sensors provide different types of information.

---

## 3. Object Detection

Object detection allows an AI system to identify objects within an image or video.

Possible detections include:

- Humans
- Vehicles
- Weapons
- Buildings
- Robots
- Animals
- Aircraft

### Fictional Application

Atlas may use distributed vision systems to identify potential threats.

---

## 4. Object Tracking

Object tracking allows a system to follow an object across multiple frames of video.

### Fictional Application

A surveillance drone could track Ethan while he moves through an abandoned city.

---

## 5. Facial Recognition

Facial-recognition systems attempt to identify or verify individuals based on facial characteristics.

### Story Application

The Resistance may use disguises, environmental interference, or destroyed surveillance infrastructure to avoid machine identification.

---

## 6. Image Classification

Image classification assigns an image to a predefined category.

Example:

A system could classify an image as:

- Human
- Robot
- Vehicle
- Building
- Weapon

---

## 7. Semantic Segmentation

Semantic segmentation assigns categories to individual pixels.

A futuristic autonomous system could use segmentation to distinguish:

- Roads
- Buildings
- Vegetation
- Humans
- Water
- Obstacles

---

## 8. Depth Perception

Depth information helps machines estimate the distance between objects.

Possible technologies:

- Stereo cameras
- LiDAR
- Time-of-flight sensors
- Structured light

---

## 9. Thermal Imaging

Thermal cameras detect infrared radiation associated with temperature.

### Fictional Application

Resistance members may attempt to avoid thermal detection by:

- Moving through insulated structures
- Using underground routes
- Exploiting environmental heat sources

---

## 10. Computer Vision in Robotics

Robots can combine vision with other sensors to navigate environments.

Possible applications:

- Navigation
- Obstacle avoidance
- Object manipulation
- Search and rescue
- Surveillance
- Autonomous transportation

---

## 11. Limitations

Computer vision systems can fail because of:

- Darkness
- Occlusion
- Weather
- Damaged sensors
- Poor image quality
- Unusual objects
- Adversarial interference
- Missing data

These limitations can create believable opportunities for characters to evade machines.

---

## 12. Story Rule

Computer vision should make machines powerful without making them magically omniscient.

Machines should have sensors, processing systems, limitations, and uncertainty.
